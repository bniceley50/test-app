# PART 1: KENTUCKY REVISED STATUTES (KRS) CHAPTER 318 - PLUMBERS AND PLUMBING

---

## KRS 318.010 - Definitions

Key definitions:

- **"Plumbing"** - The art of installing in buildings the pipes for distributing the water supply, the fixtures for using water and drainage pipes for removing waste water and sewage, together with fittings, appurtenances, and appliances of various kinds, all within or adjacent to the building. It shall not include the installation of on-site sewage disposal systems, except for the piping, fixtures, or other appurtenances needed within the building. It shall include the water service pipe which forms the connection between the property line and the building, other than piping serving firefighting equipment.

- **"Master Plumber"** - A person who assumes responsible charge, supervision, or direction of journeyman plumbers, plumbers' apprentices, and other persons in the construction, installation, or alteration of plumbing, or who engages in, offers to engage in, or advertises or otherwise represents that he is permitted or qualified to engage in the design, planning, superintending, contracting for, or responsible charge of plumbing.

- **"Farmstead"** - Property with a bona fide "agricultural land" or "horticultural land" use as defined by KRS 132.010(9) and (10).

## KRS 318.015 - Application of Chapter and of State Plumbing Code

The state plumbing code promulgated by the department shall apply to and shall be in full force and effect for all public buildings.

## KRS 318.020 - Apprentices, Maintenance Men, Water Company or District Employees Exempt

- Nothing in the chapter shall prohibit the employment of an apprentice to assist a journeyman plumber.
- The chapter shall not apply to anyone employed or acting as a maintenance man.
- The chapter shall not apply to persons employed by any water company or water district.

## KRS 318.030 - License and General Liability Insurance Required

- No person, firm, or corporation shall engage in plumbing unless maintaining general liability insurance in an amount not less than $250,000.
- Must submit proof of compliance with workers' compensation and unemployment insurance laws.
- Proof of insurance shall be submitted to the department prior to issuance or renewal of an active master plumber license.
- Insurance carriers shall notify the department upon cancellation.
- Insurance requirement does not apply to an employee of a person engaged in plumbing.

## KRS 318.040 - Qualifications - Examinations - Reciprocity

**Qualifications:**
- Be at least 18 years of age
- Be of good moral character
- Be a citizen of the US or a resident alien authorized to work in the United States
- Possess all other qualifications prescribed by administrative regulations

**Examinations:**
- No master or journeyman plumber's license shall be issued except upon successful passage of an examination.
- Examinations consist of written and practical tests.
- Examination papers shall be preserved for one (1) year.

**Reciprocity:**
- The department may issue a license to a person holding a valid license in another state if that state has a statewide plumbing code and the examination is deemed at least equal to Kentucky's and the other state agrees to reciprocate.

## KRS 318.120 - Employment of Unlicensed Plumber Prohibited

No person or corporation who assumes responsible charge and direction of other persons in the installation of plumbing shall employ any person not licensed under Chapter 318 to perform and install plumbing.

## KRS 318.130 - Kentucky State Plumbing Code - Administrative Regulations

Directs the department to promulgate (and amend) a code known as the Kentucky State Plumbing Code, regulating the construction, installation, and alteration of plumbing and plumbing fixtures and appliances, house sewers, and private water supplies.

## KRS 318.134 - Installation Permits - Requisites - Fees

- All applications must be accompanied by plans and specifications.
- If an on-site sewage disposal system without a surface discharge is proposed, a valid permit from the Cabinet for Health and Family Services is required.
- The department fixes a reasonable schedule of fees and charges.

## KRS 318.140 - Adoption and Enforcement by Local Governments

- Two or more local governments may jointly enact and enforce the plumbing code.
- The department may authorize local governments to administer, carry out, and enforce the code.
- No person shall be eligible for appointment as a local government plumbing inspector unless having at least 8 years' experience as a master or journeyman plumber.

## KRS 318.160 - Installation Plans and Specifications

A person who constructs, installs, or extensively alters any plumbing, sewerage, or water supply system of any public building shall obtain approval of the department in writing.

## KRS 318.170 - Authority of Agents (Inspections & Stop Orders)

- Officers, agents, and inspectors may enter premises at all reasonable times for inspections.
- They may interrogate persons and require production of plumbing installation permits.
- They are empowered to issue stop orders when plumbing is found in violation.

## KRS 318.165 - Requisites for Permanent Water Supply

Interior plumbing must be installed and approved before a permanent water supply connection can be provided.

## KRS 318.190 - Jurisdiction for Enforcement Actions

- Circuit Court where the violation occurs has jurisdiction in civil/injunctive actions.
- Franklin Circuit Court holds concurrent jurisdiction.
- District Court where the violation occurs has jurisdiction in criminal actions.

## KRS 318.990 - Penalties

- Fine not less than $10 nor more than $100, or imprisonment for not more than 90 days, or both, for each offense.
- Each day the violation continues constitutes a separate offense.

## 815 KAR 20:010 - Definitions for 815 KAR Chapter 20

Over 200 defined terms. Key definitions include:

1. **ABS** - Acrylonitrile-butadiene styrene
2. **Administrative authority** - The Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction or any authorized person/agency
3. **Air break** - A piping arrangement for a drainage system in which a drain discharges indirectly into another fixture at a point below the flood level rim
4. **Air gap (drainage)** - The unobstructed vertical distance through the free atmosphere between the outlet of waste pipe and the flood level rim of the receptacle
5. **Air gap (water distribution)** - The unobstructed vertical distance through the free atmosphere between the lowest opening from any pipe or faucet supplying water and the flood level rim of the receptacle
6. **ANSI** - American National Standards Institute
7. **APML** - Approved Parts or Materials List as established in 815 KAR 20:020, Section 4
8. **Approved** - Accepted or acceptable under an applicable specification stated or cited in this code
9. **Area drain** - A receptacle designed to collect surface or storm water from an open area
10. **Aspirator** - A fitting or device supplied with water under positive pressure passing through an integral orifice causing a vacuum
11. **Backflow** - The flow of water or other liquids into the distributing pipes of a potable supply from any source other than its intended source
12. **Existing work** - A plumbing system or part installed prior to the effective date of the applicable provision
13. **Farm** - Property with a bona fide agricultural or horticultural land use as defined by KRS 132.010(9) and (10)
14. **Fire line** - A system of pipes and equipment used exclusively to supply water for extinguishing fires
15. **Fixture** - "Plumbing fixture" as defined by this administrative regulation
16. **Fixture branch** - The piping distance between a soil, waste, and vent stack and the fixture trap
17. **Fixture drain** - The drain from the trap of a fixture to the junction of that drain with any other drain pipe
18. **Fixture supply** - The water supply pipe connecting a fixture to a branch water supply pipe or main water supply pipe
19. **Plumbing inspector** - A duly authorized employee or agent of the DHBC charged with inspecting plumbing installations and enforcing the Kentucky State Plumbing Code
20. **Plumbing repair** - Replacing a part or putting together a part or parts torn or broken
21. **Plumbing system** - Appliances and water heaters; water supply distributing pipes; fixtures and fixture traps; soil, waste, and vent pipes; house drain and house sewer; storm water drainage within a building
22. **Pool** - "Swimming pool" as defined by this administrative regulation
23. **Medical gas system** - An assembly of equipment and piping for the distribution of nonflammable medical gases (oxygen, nitrous oxide, compressed air, carbon dioxide, helium)

## 815 KAR 20:020 - Parts or Materials List (APML)

### Section 1 - Definitions
- "ABS" means acrylonitrile-butadiene-styrene pipe
- "APML" means the Approved Parts or Materials List
- "ASTM" means American Society for Testing Materials
- "Parts or materials" means all types of fittings and piping used in soil, waste and vent systems, house sewers, potable water supply, plumbing fixtures, appurtenances, and mechanical sewage systems

### Section 2 - Approved Parts or Materials List (APML)
- A part or material manufactured per a specification listed in the code shall be considered approved if it meets the latest edition of the specification.
- A part or material shall not be used unless currently authorized by the code or considered by the committee.

### Section 3 - Amending the APML
- A person may petition the committee in writing no later than 14 days prior to the committee's next meeting.
- The committee shall forward recommendations within 30 days to the department.

### Section 4 - Custody
- The Director, Division of Plumbing, shall maintain the APML and make it available during regular office hours.
- Copies may be obtained by mailing to the Division of Plumbing, 101 Sea Hero Road, Frankfort, KY 40601-5405.

### Section 5 - Approved Items (Selected)

- Oatey 18" x 18" no caulk thermoplastic flashing
- Envirovac Vacuum Systems Condensate Collection System

## 815 KAR 20:030 - Plumbing Licenses

### Section 1 - Examinations
- Regular examinations: February, May, August, and November
- Maximum 2.5 hours for written portion; 2.5 hours for practical portion
- Content: Written questions on basic principles, KRS Chapter 318, 815 KAR Chapters 2 and 20; pipe sizing on drawings; practical demonstration (journeyman)
- Applicant for journeyman exam shall furnish plumbing materials for practical examination
- Department notifies applicant at least 1 week prior regarding needed materials
- Master exam is more complex than journeyman exam
- Failing: Application void if not completed within 1 year
- Passing score validity: 3 years

### Section 2 - Master Plumber License
**Qualifications (one of):**
- (a) Held valid journeyman license for minimum 2 years within past 5 years AND actively employed under supervision of master plumber for minimum 2 years
- (b) Hold an engineer license in Kentucky with sufficient mechanical engineering experience
- (c) Hold valid plumbing license from another state for minimum 2 years within past 5 years
- (d) 4 consecutive years in equivalent capacity in a non-licensing state

**Passing Score:** Minimum 80% on each portion
**License Fee:** $250
**Fee Proration:** Not less than 7 months or more than 18 months; expires on final day of birth month

### Section 3 - Journeyman Plumber License
**Qualifications:**
- At least 2 consecutive years as apprentice, OR
- Department-approved course + at least 1 year as apprentice

**Passing Score:** Minimum 75% on each portion
**License Fee:** $60
**Fee Proration:** Same as master

### Section 4 - Renewals
- Master renewal: $250; Journeyman renewal: $60
- Must complete continuing education (815 KAR 2:010)
- Master must show proof of insurance (KRS 318.030)
- Inactive fees: Master $125; Journeyman $30
- Inactive master shall not secure permits, advertise, or engage in master plumber work

## 815 KAR 20:050 - Installation Permits

### Section 1 - Permit Required For:
- Existing installation if fixture, soil, or waste opening is moved/relocated
- New house sewer or replacement
- New water service or replacement

**Who May Obtain:**
- Licensed master plumbers
- Homeowners (for personal residences only)

**Homeowner Permit Conditions:**
1. Application made prior to beginning work
2. Homeowner files affidavit
3. All work compliant with 815 KAR Chapter 20
4. All work personally performed by homeowner
5. Homeowner not obtained another new-home permit within last 5 years

**Journeyman plumber:** Shall not install plumbing unless under supervision of a licensed master plumber with a valid permit.

### Section 3 - Plumbing Plan Submission
- Plans shall be submitted for review and approval prior to permit issuance
- Field inspection without prior plan submission: allowed for no more than 10 openings
- Inspector shall NOT review plans for projects on private water systems or sewer main extensions without Division of Water approval

### Section 4 - Permit Fees

**Residential (1 & 2 Family):**
- Base fee: $50
- Plus $14 per fixture/appliance/opening

**Commercial/Other:**
- Base fee: $50
- Plus $20 per fixture/appliance/opening

**Water Heater Only:**
- Single water heater: $50 total (only fee)

**Base Fee Only:**
- Work not including new installation
- Corrections or testing for someone else's installation
- Master plumber taking over another permit

### Section 5 - Inspections
- 5 plumbing inspections at no additional cost per permit

### Section 6 - Expiration
- Permits expire 1 year after issuance unless construction is ongoing

## 815 KAR 20:055 - Water Heating Devices

### Section 1 - Installation Requirements

**Space Heating:** Hot water for domestic/commercial purposes may be used for space heating if temperature requirements are met.

**Fuel-Fired Water Heaters:**
- Shall be connected to a flue or chimney
- Flue shall be at least as large as required by manufacturer's instructions
- Flue shall extend 2 feet above the roof
- Flue shall not terminate within 6 feet of a door or window
- Vent shall NOT be connected to a flue serving a coal-burning apparatus
- Shall not be placed in a closed room/closet unless ventilated per National Fuel Gas Code

**Gas-Fired Direct Vent:**
- 50,000 BTU/hr or less: vent terminal at least 9 inches from any building opening
- Over 50,000 BTU/hr: vent terminal at least 12 inches from any building opening

**Attic, Drop Ceiling, or Wood Flooring (effective April 1, 2021):**
- Must install corrosion-resistant watertight pan below heater
- Pan must have at least 3/4" drain, piped similarly to T&P relief valve discharge
- T&P valve may discharge into drain pan if pan pipe discharges through air gap

**Condensate Drain:**
- Shall not drain into crawl space, sewer, vent stack, or area subject to freezing

### Section 5 - Temperature and Pressure Relief Devices
- Only devices approved per 815 KAR 20:020 and 815 KAR 20:120 shall be installed
- T&P relief device shall be installed on hot water side, not more than 3 inches from top of water heater
- Installed per manufacturer's recommendation if marked opening is provided

### Section 7 - Reporting
- Form PLB-94 "Water Heater Report Form" within 30 days of sale
- Installer with installation permit is exempt from reporting

## 815 KAR 20:060 - Quality, Weight, Installation, and Storage of Materials

### Section 1 - Quality and Handling
- Material shall be free of defects
- Materials installed per manufacturer's recommendations unless otherwise stated

### Commingling and Mixing
- Plastic pipe shall not be commingled except through male/female adapters or approved transition fittings
- Plastic and metal piping shall discharge into one another using proper fittings/adapters

### Thermal Expansion
- Each installation shall be engineered giving due consideration to expansion characteristics

### Storage
- Solvent cement and primers: not exposed to ignition, sparks, open flames, or heat; not used beyond shelf life
- PEX, brass, copper: not exposed to materials affecting basic properties

### Section 3 - Marking
- Each pipe, fitting, trap, fixture, or device shall be indelibly marked with maker's mark/name

### Section 4 - Pipe Standards
- Vitrified clay pipe: ASTM C700, fittings ASTM C425
- Concrete pipe: ASTM C14, fittings ASTM C443
- Truss pipe: ASTM D2680 (solid wall: ASTM D2751)
- Extra heavy SDR 35 sewer piping: ASTM D3034

### Joints
- Cast iron: caulked, screwed, or neoprene gaskets (ASTM C564)
- Steel, brass, copper to cast iron: screwed or caulked
- Hubless cast iron coupling: neoprene gasket + cast iron clamps (ASTM A48) + stainless steel bolts/nuts (ANSI B 18.2.1 and B 18.2.2)

### Section 10 - CPVC Pipe
- Clean with CPVC cleaner per manufacturer's recommendations unless using approved one-step cement

### Installation in Trenches
- Trenches dug too deep: piping supported with approved grillage on firm ground
- Piping on grade: secured to prevent misalignment during slab pouring
- Closet bends under slabs: adequately secured

## 815 KAR 20:070 - Plumbing Fixtures

### Section 1 - Materials

- Installed to allow access for cleaning

- PP lining inside

### Section 5 - Frost-proof Closet
- Only in buildings with at least 12-inch air break between it and any habitable building

### Section 6 - Floor and Shower Drains
- Considered plumbing fixtures, shall have strainers
- Shower drain pan: minimum 6 inches on all vertical walls

### Section 9 - Fixture Additions
- Must comply with all applicable code sections

### Section 10 - Defective Fixtures
- Shall be repaired, replaced, or removed within 30 days upon written notice

### Section 11 - Conservation of Water

- **Exceptions:** Standard flush toilets/urinals allowed if drainage system configuration requires greater water quantity, or owner requests antique fixtures

## 815 KAR 20:090 - Soil, Waste, Vent Systems, Traps, and Clean-Outs

### Section 1 - Materials
- Main or branch soil, waste, and vent pipes within/underneath a building: hub and spigot extra heavy or service weight cast iron, among other approved materials
- Tubular or schedule 40 PVC/ABS p-trap: union-joint or solvent welded type
- Tubular brass trap: seventeen (17) gauge

### Section 2 - Trap Requirements
- Each fixture separately trapped by water-seal trap as near as possible to fixture
- Maximum distance from bottom of fixture: 10 inches
- Fixture trap vent (except water closet): not below the dip of the trap
- Each 90-degree turn in the waste line shall be washed
- Fixture branch on water closet: not more than 4 feet 6 inches

### Section 3 - Clean-Out Requirements
- Clean-out ferrule bodies: standard pipe size, conforming in thickness to pipe/fittings
- Shall not extend less than 1/4 inch
- Sufficient cleanouts for thorough cleaning

### Section 4 - Grades and Supports of Horizontal Piping
(Addresses grades and supports for horizontal piping)

### Section 5 - Change in Direction
- Made by 45-degree wye, half-wye, quarter bend, or similar approved fittings

### Section 11 - Soil or Waste and Vent Stacks
- Building with fixtures shall have soil/waste and vent stack(s) extending full size through the roof
- Stack shall be as direct as possible, free from sharp bends/turns
- Size determined by total fixture units connected
- More than 3 water closets (3.5 gpf) shall not discharge into a 3" stack
- More than 4 water closets (1.6 gpf or less) shall not discharge into a 3" stack

### Section 12 - Future Openings
- Existing/future openings shall be complete with soil, waste, and vent piping

### Section 13 - House Drain
- Size determined by total fixture units connecting to house drain
- Total area of vents through roof shall equal house drain area, minimum one 3" stack

### Section 15 - Changing Pipes in Existing Buildings
- Replace with appropriate size/materials for new work if fixtures changed/replaced and piping not extended through roof

### Section 16 - Prohibited Connections
- No fixture connection to lead bend or branch of water closet
- Vent pipe above highest fixture shall not be used as soil/waste pipe

### Section 17 - Roof Extensions
- Full size at least 1 foot above the roof

### Overflow Pipes
- Tank/exhaust waste not directly connected to house drain, soil, or waste pipe
- Discharge upon roof or into trapped open receptacle

### Acid and Chemical Wastes
- Corrosive liquid not discharged into soil/waste/sewer system unless diluted/neutralized through proper pit

### Laboratory Waste Piping
- Sized per this regulation; each fixture individually trapped

### Grease Traps
- Restaurant/food service grease trap required per 902 KAR 10:085 if using private sewage system

### Slip Joints
- Permitted on inlet side of trap
- Single 1-1/2" slip joint with elastomeric gasket permitted on outlet side of 1-1/2" trap

### Special Vents
- Flat vent allowed if building design prohibits standard venting

## 815 KAR 20:120 - Water Supply and Distribution

### Section 1 - Quality
- Bacteriological and chemical quality shall comply with department regulations and Division of Water KAR Title 401
- Toxic material kept out of potable water system
- All surfaces in contact with potable water: nontoxic material
- Potable water accessible for drinking, bathing, culinary use, and food/pharmaceutical processing
- System designed to prevent contamination through cross connections
- No cross connection between private and public water supply

### Closed Water Systems - Pressure Protection (Section 1(6))
- Single check valve: thermal expansion tank or pressure relief device near water heater
- Backflow preventer: properly sized thermal expansion tank or relief device
- Pressure reducing valve without bypass: thermal expansion tank or relief device on cold water line near heater

### Section 1(7) - Backflow and Back Siphonage
- Protection required per paragraphs (a) through (l)
- Air gap provides best protection in all backflow situations
- Minimum air gap measured vertically from lowest end of potable water outlet to flood rim

### Section 3 - Water Service Pipe Separation
- Minimum 5 feet horizontal separation from house sewer (undisturbed/compacted earth)
- Same trench allowed if: water service bottom at least 18 inches above sewer top; on solid shelf; minimum joints
- At intersections: sleeve on water service extending 5 feet each side

### Section 4 - Distribution
- Water supply through piping independent of other systems
- Piping used for other purposes shall NOT convey potable water
- Nonpotable water may flush water closets/urinals if independently piped
- Dual system: nonpotable supply durably identified
- Nonpotable outlets for drinking/domestic use: permanently posted "DANGER - UNSAFE WATER"

### Section 9 - Pipes and Fittings
- Fittings: brass, copper, approved plastic, galvanized cast iron, or galvanized malleable iron
- Previously used piping/fittings not for water distribution
- Joints: screw, solder, or plastic joint; cast iron may be caulked, screwed, or machine drawn

### Section 14 - Temperature & Pressure Relief Devices
- With floor drain: discharge piped within 2 inches of floor
- Without floor drain: discharge piped to outside, ell turned down, within 4 inches of ground
- Relief device required on pneumatic water systems

### Section 20 - Fire Protection Systems
- Fire protection using potable water: equipped with 2 check valves (1 may be alarm check valve)

### Section 21 - Mobile Home Water Distribution
- Adequate and safe water supply to each mobile home
- Individual connection at appropriate location for each space
- Riser at least 4 inches above ground with 2-3/4" valve outlets with screw connection

### 2020 Code Changes
- Tracer wire required on water service
- If plastic piping transitions to copper before building, tracer wire bonded to copper piping

## 815 KAR 20:130 - House Sewers and Storm Water Piping; Methods of Installation

### Section 1 - Independent System
- New building drainage/plumbing system shall be separate and independent
- Each building shall have independent connection with public or private sewer

### Section 2 - Exceptions
- Rear building sewer may be carried through front building under specific conditions

### Section 3 - Private Sewage Disposal
- If no sewer available, house drain connects to approved private sewage disposal system

### Section 4 - Excavations
- Open trench work; trenches kept open until piping inspected

### Section 5 - Storm/House Sewers in Undisturbed or Filled Ground
- House sewer in undisturbed ground: at least 4 inches of pea gravel, sand, or approved grillage
- Storm sewer in undisturbed ground: no grillage required
- Storm/house sewer in filled ground: embedded to lower quadrant with at least 4" concrete pad below invert
- Support in filled ground: on 10-foot center to solid footing
- Flexible thermoplastic sewer pipe: at least 6 inches of gravel on bottom, top, and sides
- Backfill by hand, tamped 6 inches above piping or filled with 6" grillage above
- Less than 18 inches of cover: written variance request required

### Section 6 - New House Sewer Connections
- May connect to house drain if existing system meets the code

### Section 7 - House Sewer Materials
- Type PS 46 PVC (4"-15", ASTM F789)
- PVC ribbed pipe (ASTM F794)
- PE (ASTM F714)
- Other listed materials

### Section 8 - Storm Sewers Inside Buildings
- Extending 2 feet outside building in sizes 8" and smaller: approved materials

### Section 10 - House Sewer Sizing
- Sized same manner as house drain
- House drains per 815 KAR 20:090

### Section 11 - Storm Systems
- Size determined by total drained area in horizontal projection
- Shall not be laid parallel to or within 2 feet of a bearing wall
- Laid at sufficient depth to protect from freezing

### Section 12 - Combined Storm and Sanitary Sewer
- Size determined by multiplying fixture units by conversion factor + drained area, applied to storm water table

### Section 14 - Drainage Below Sewer Level (Public Buildings)
- Sewage lifted by approved device and discharged into building sewer

### Section 20 - Drainage of Yards, Areas, Roofs, and Traps
- Drain not connected to combined sewer: no trap required

### Section 22 - Inside Conductors/Roof Leaders
- PVC or ABS limited to buildings where conductor does not exceed 45 feet in height
- Limited to Schedule 40 or 80 (PVC: ASTM D2665/D1784; ABS: ASTM D2661)
- Installed per manufacturer's recommendations

### Section 26 - Overflow Pipes
- Overflow from cistern, supply tank, expansion tank, or drip pan connects indirectly

### Section 27 - Subsoil Drains
- Below sewer level: discharge into sump/receiving tank, automatically lifted

### Section 28 - New Sewer Connections to Existing Buildings
- Existing basement floor drain/sump pump must comply with construction requirements and be inspected

## 815 KAR 20:150 - Inspection and Tests

### Section 1 - Required Inspections and Tests
- Rough-in inspection: prior to covering or concealment
- Final inspection and air test: complete plumbing system
- Tests: separately or combined; house sewer and branches from property line to house drain
- If covered before inspection: shall be uncovered
- Condemned buildings: alterations considered new plumbing system
- Alterations >10 feet of soil, waste, or vent piping: inspected and tested

### Section 2 - Permit Holder Requirements
- Furnish all equipment, material, and labor
- Notify department and request rough-in inspection before concealment
- Notify and request final inspection upon completion

### Section 3 - Remote Inspection
- Master plumber may request live video or recorded video inspection
- Requires certificate of completion (valid 3 years)
- Training covers: technology, information requirements, corrections
- Denied if scope too complex: multiple visit underground installations, multi-inspection rough-ins, new construction finals
- Remote inspection seals: Form PLB-4 and PLB-5

### Section 4 - Testing of Systems

**(1) Water Distribution System:**
- Tested with air or water at not less than maximum working pressure

**(2) Water Test:**
- All openings closed except highest; filled to point of overflow
- Sections: plugged except highest, tested with not less than 10-foot head of water
- Successive sections: upper 10 feet of preceding section retested

**(3) Air Pressure Test:**
- In lieu of water test: forced air to 5 PSI uniform pressure

**(4) Final Air Test:**
- After fixtures set and traps filled with water, before occupancy
- Air pressure equivalent to 1-inch water column
- Maintained for at least 15 minutes
- No leaks or forcing of trap seals indicates airtight system

**(5) Garage Drainage:**
- Tested same as soil, waste, and vent system

**(6) House Sewer:**
- Tested by water, air, or smoke test
- 4-inch test tee or Y connection at property line

**(7) Clean-out Removal:**
- Department may require removal to verify pressure reached all parts

**(8) Building Sewer Not Drained by Gravity:**
- Minimum 24 inches of cover
- Tested at 5 PSI for 15 minutes

**(9) Rain Water Conductors:**
- Inside conductors: water, air, or smoke test

### Section 5 - Defective Work
- Replaced; inspection and test repeated

### Section 6 - Testing Defective Plumbing
- Air test used for suspected defective systems

### Section 7 - Certificate of Approval
- Issued upon satisfactory completion and final test

## 815 KAR 20:170 - Manufactured Home and Mobile Home Community Systems

### Community Waste Systems
- Main/branch sewers: uniform grade minimum 1/8 inch per foot
- All joints water tight
- Clean-outs at intervals not exceeding 100 feet for 6" and smaller
- Extended to grade with cast-iron soil pipe or schedule 40 ABS/PVC

### Individual Connections
- Each home: 4-inch sewer
- 3-inch waste connection, extended 1 inch above grade, 3" standard female thread
- 4-inch concrete pad, 24 inches square, around waste opening
- 3-inch screw plug fastened by chain to concrete pad
- Waste pipe connection: waterproof, cast-iron, schedule 40 steel, copper, or schedule 40 ABS/PVC

### Water Distribution
- Adequate and safe water supply to each home
- Individual connection at appropriate location for each space
- Riser at least 4 inches above ground, minimum 2 three-fourths (3/4) inch valves
- Shutoff valve may be below frost depth but shall NOT be a stop-and-waste cock

## 815 KAR 20:180 - Special Connections

### Section 1 - Commercial Laundry Wastes
- Not discharged into open trench unless drained into at least 4" trap with full-size vent
- Trench constructed of material resistant to alkaline waste

### Section 2 - Semicommercial Laundries (Automatic)
- Discharge into 4" waste line for washing machines only
- Full-size vent; base of stack washed by machine or starch sink
- 4" trap serving not more than 2 washing machines

### Section 3 - Washing Machines, Residential (New Buildings)
- 2" trap, vented per 815 KAR 20:080
- Trap installed 12 inches above floor with 2" stand pipe to washer height
- Private disposal system: add 50 feet additional lateral

### Section 4 - Washing Machines, Residential (Old Buildings)
- Connected via 4" cast-iron P-trap on outside of building
- Trap has vented cover extending 3 inches above grade

### Section 5 - Air Conditioning Equipment
- Shall conform with 815 KAR 20:090, Sections 29 and 32
- Evaporative cooler/air washer/air handling equipment: no direct connection to soil, waste, or vent pipe
- Drained by indirect waste pipe through air gap or air break into open floor sink/drain/receptor
- Air gap required if indirect waste pipe may be under vacuum

### Section 6 - Garage Sand Trap
- Constructed of concrete with heavy cover or grate

### Section 7 - Hot Water, Steam Blowoffs
- Discharge into tank/basin with airtight cover and 4" vent independent of other venting

### Section 8 - Pedicure Chairs
- 2" open receptacle for up to 2 chairs
- 3" open receptacle for 3-6 chairs
- Below flood level rim or spray hose: high hazard - requires reduced pressure principle backflow preventer on hot and cold supply

## 815 KAR 20:191 - Minimum Fixture Requirements

### Section 1 - General Requirements
- Male/female occupancy: presumed equally divided unless denoted otherwise
- Occupancy load factor: per Kentucky Building Code (815 KAR 7:120)
- Toilet rooms on each level/floor (or every other level if sufficient)
- Historic buildings (National Register, 100 persons or less): may use unisex facilities, no urinals required
- Urinal substitution: may substitute for water closet (males) if substituted urinals do not exceed 1/3 of required water closets

### Section 2 - Toilet Floor Construction
- Nonabsorbent materials; wood floors covered by nonabsorbent materials

### Section 3 - Fixture Requirements Chart
(Occupancy-based chart by Assembly, Business, Educational, Factory, Institutional, Mercantile, Residential, Storage groups - see official PDF)

### Section 4 - Stages
- Separate water closet and lavatory for males and females
- Drinking fountain in stage and auditorium area

### Section 5 - Theaters, Assembly Halls
- Over 400 males: 3 water closets + 1 per additional 500 males
- Over 600 males: 3 urinals + 1 per additional 300 males

### Section 6 - Restaurants and Food Establishments
- 1-15 persons: comply with specified requirements or 1 unisex facility (1 WC + 1 lavatory)
- Over 300 persons: 4 WC + 1 per additional 200 persons
- Carry-out: exempt from patron toilet facilities
- Handwashing sinks: minimum 100 deg F, maximum 120 deg F hot water
- 3-compartment utensil sink: minimum 2" drain, direct connection
- Dishwashing machines: indirect discharge through 3" open receptacle
- Hub drains/open receptacles/floor sinks: extend 1 inch above floor plane unless full grate flush

### Section 7 - Schools (K-12)
- Modular classrooms: no facilities required if:
  - Elementary (through 5th): within 100 feet of main structure
  - 6th and above: within 200 feet
- Water closets: elongated bowl with split open front seat
- Minimum 2 water bottle filling stations per school
- Minimum 1 drinking fountain or bottle filling station per floor/wing
- Minimum 1 drinking fountain or bottle filling station per 75 students

### Section 8 - Higher Education
- 1 WC per 50 males; 1 WC per 25 females
- 1 lavatory per 50 persons
- 1 drinking fountain per 75 persons

### Section 9 - Other Requirements
- Dormitories (R-2): 1 laundry tray or washer per 50 persons
- Transient lodging with private bath: 1 bathtub/shower per room
- Dormitories/fraternities/sororities: 1 bathtub/shower per 8 persons (up to 150), then 1 per 20 persons
- Private residential bath: 1 WC, 1 lavatory, 1 bathtub/shower
- Apartments/condos: kitchen type sink required
- Waiting rooms (I-2, I-3): at least 1 WC and 1 lavatory
- Over 5 employees: separate facilities for each sex

**Fixture Alternatives:**
- 18" of circular sink = 1 lavatory
- 24" of trough urinal = 1 urinal

### Section 16 - Park Service Buildings / Bathhouses
- 1-15 vehicle spaces: Males: 1 WC, 1 urinal, 1 lavatory, 1 shower; Females: 1 WC, 1 lavatory, 1 shower
- 16-30 spaces: Males: 1 WC, 1 urinal, 2 lavatories, 2 showers; Females: 2 WC, 2 lavatories, 2 showers
- 31-45 spaces: Males: 2 WC, 1 urinal, 3 lavatories, 3 showers; Females: 2 WC, 3 lavatories, 3 showers
- 46-60 spaces: Males: 2 WC, 2 urinals, 3 lavatories, 3 showers; Females: 3 WC, 3 lavatories, 3 showers
- 61-80 spaces: Males: 3 WC, 2 urinals, 4 lavatories, 4 showers; Females: 4 WC, 4 lavatories, 4 showers
- 81-100 spaces: Males: 4 WC, 2 urinals, 5 lavatories, 5 showers; Females: 5 WC, 5 lavatories, 5 showers
- Over 100: +1 WC and lavatory per sex per 30 spaces; +1 shower per sex per 40 spaces; +1 urinal per 100 spaces

### Section 17 - Residential/Day Camp Sites
- Scaled similarly by persons served
- 34-48 persons: Males: 2 WC, 2 urinals, 2 lavatories, 3 showers; Females: 3 WC, 2 lavatories, 3 showers
- 49-63 persons: Males: 3 WC, 2 urinals, 3 lavatories, 4 showers; Females: 4 WC, 3 lavatories, 4 showers

### Mobile Food Units
- Not within existing permitted establishment: 1 unisex facility (1 WC + 1 lavatory)

# PART 3: BASIC PRINCIPLES OF THE NATIONAL PLUMBING CODE

- **Principle No. 21 - Structural Safety:** Plumbing shall be installed with due regard to preservation of the strength of structural members and prevention of damage to walls and other surfaces.
- **Principle No. 22 - Protect Ground and Surface Water:** Sewage or other waste shall not be discharged into surface or sub-surface water unless subjected to acceptable treatment.
